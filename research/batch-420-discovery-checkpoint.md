## Review method

The complete source page was inspected from a 150 dpi render before research. The ten rows were then checked against the complete 9,200,232-row Army Serial Number Merged File, its fixed-width technical documentation, and rendered NARA occupational-code pages. Exact names, meaningful variants, private identifiers where the index supplies them, OSS and CIA context, employer and occupation terms, obituaries, institutional sources, current Library of Congress discovery, and broader archival discovery were reviewed in stages. Search snippets, genealogy-only pages, modern people finders, and name-only biographies were not accepted as final evidence.

No raw Army row, full private identifier, authenticated API payload, home address, or family-sensitive detail is retained here. The user-supplied NARA API key was not used.

## Source-specific cautions

- The Army merged file is incomplete, especially for officers. A missing row is not negative proof.
- Army occupation codes 001, 002, 227 and 726 identify broad categories, not employers, workplaces or exact jobs.
- The Army file's `SND` text following Eugene H Clapp's name is preserved without guessing that it is a suffix.
- James W Clance's rare name, Florida National Guard corporal grade and World War II paratrooper trajectory make a strong candidate, but the identifier conflict controls. The records remain unmerged.
- Frederick Gardner Clapp's well-documented petroleum-geology career is not assigned to the indexed person because the reviewed sources add no OSS connection or second corroborating identifier.
- The sixteen Albert L Clark Army rows remain sixteen separate candidates. No convenient occupation or biography is selected.
- Alvin Clark's exact identifier supports the Army name variant with middle initial C. Private at Army entry and T-5 in the index are different observations, not a contradiction or automatic chronology.
- An OSS personnel-file index row establishes that a file exists, not by itself the person's exact role or the chronology of every outside record.
