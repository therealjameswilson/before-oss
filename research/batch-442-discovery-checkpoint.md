## Review method

PDF pages 86 and 87 were rendered at 150 dpi and visually inspected before
research. Every printed name, initial, blank rank, private identifier or blank,
box, note and location in the ten-row cohort was compared with the immutable
database. Five printed identifiers remain research-only and are masked in every
public output. The six-digit Frederic W Cole and seven-digit Nathan L Cole
values were preserved literally; neither was padded or assigned to a service-
number system to manufacture a match.

The complete official Army merged file was scanned across 9,200,232 fixed-
width records. Literal private-identifier comparisons select only Manny Cole
and Raymond T Cole. Manny's identifier selects the damaged Army-file name
`COLE MJNNY`; the damage remains visible as a documented variant rather than a
silent correction. The row dates Army entry to 27 January 1944, carries private
grade code 8 and civilian-occupation value 158. Raymond's identifier selects
one of two exact-name Army rows, dates Army entry to 29 February 1944 as an Air
Corps private and carries occupation value 001.

The official NARA record-layout and code-list documents were reviewed before
those fields were interpreted. Value 158 is the historical category
`Newsboys`; value 001 is `Accountants and auditors`. For readable presentation
the first is displayed as `Newsboy` and the second as `Accountant or auditor`,
while the exact official labels remain in the evidence. Both are modeled as
medium-confidence, strongly date-bounded occupation observations, not
employers. No newspaper, route, firm, client, industry, workplace or exact
relationship to OSS service is inferred.

The Army file produced no literal identifier match for John O Cole, although
three exact-name Army rows have other identifiers. It produced no exact-name
row for Frederic W Cole; one for Gordon H Cole; six for Joe Cole; one for
Joseph G Cole; none for Kathryn B Cole; one for Nathan L Cole; and no Army row
for Remsen J Cole. None of those name-only candidates was selected. The Army
file is incomplete and is neither an officer, Navy, Marine Corps nor
comprehensive OSS roster, so a non-hit is not negative proof.

An official Marine Corps history preserved by the Government Publishing Office
lists `Remsen J. Cole` among Marine lieutenant colonels who served with OSS and
gives Washington as his area. The exact uncommon three-part name plus explicit
OSS context supports a high-confidence identity and commissioned Marine Corps
classification. It does not reach `confirmed` because the roster is a later
official compilation rather than a direct personnel-file or service-number
link, and the author describes the roster as incomplete.

A contemporary author description for Remsen J. Cole's September 1940 article
in *Public Opinion Quarterly* says that he headed `Remsen J. Cole and
Associates`, a Philadelphia public-relations concern. The rare-name identity is
independently anchored to OSS service by the Marine roster. The eponymous firm
is therefore published as a high-confidence self-employment affiliation and
the historical organization name is preserved. Because no commission,
service-entry or OSS-transfer date has been established, the 1940 role is
`documented_prewar`, not immediate pre-OSS and not last civilian employment.

Gordon H Cole's labor-press editor lead was rejected because it has no private
identifier, Box 134, military or OSS bridge. Common-name biographies,
directories, obituaries, cemetery records and genealogy results for the other
people were rejected when they lacked the corroborating identifiers required
to distinguish the indexed person. Search snippets, unsourced genealogy,
modern people finders and living-person contact data were not used as final
evidence or retained.

Research for every person covered official NARA and CIA context, exact-name
OSS searches and meaningful variants, employment and occupation queries,
institutional archives, obituaries, directories, newspapers, current Library
of Congress discovery and other archival searches. The user-supplied NARA
Catalog API credential was not used. No raw Army row, full private identifier,
live API response, home address, phone number, living-relative detail or
unrelated sensitive data is retained.

## Source-specific cautions

- The Army merged file is incomplete and is neither an officer, Navy, Marine
  Corps nor comprehensive OSS register. Non-hits are not negative proof.
- A short printed identifier is not silently padded, truncated or assigned to
  an identifier system merely to select a candidate.
- Historical Army-entry occupation categories identify no employer and do not
  establish whether the recorded work immediately preceded OSS service.
- The Army-file `Mjnny` spelling is retained as a damaged documented variant;
  the private identifier, not name similarity alone, supports the match.
- The official Marine roster supplies a high-confidence identity, rank and OSS
  context, but it is not a direct personnel-file linkage and gives no service
  dates.
- The 1940 journal establishes Remsen Cole's named firm and profession, not the
  date he entered the Marine Corps or whether an intervening role existed.
- Blank index ranks remain blank. Dated Army-entry private grades and the later
  Marine roster rank are separate observations, not rewrites of the index.
