## Body-level lead to adjudicate

The strongest French lead is the Colonel Jean Chrétien identified in George C.
Chalou's NARA-published 1992 conference volume *The Secrets War*. Arthur L.
Funk's North Africa chapter states that OSS cooperated with Col. Jean Chrétien
and other French intelligence officers; the relevant passage appears on
printed page 167 of the inspected scan. The official NARA publications page
establishes the volume's title, editor, publisher and provenance. Exact name,
rank, the index's French note and direct OSS context support a high-confidence
identity. A fuller biographical lead and French military-file reference remain
unpublished because no personnel-file identifier yet connects them to Box 122.

An official NARA JFK-release PDF contains a CIA cable from Shanghai dated
1946-08-30 that names Anthony M. Chrisler among newly appointed radio operators.
The exact full name and intelligence-successor context make it a useful identity
lead, but the document is postwar and does not establish a pre-OSS employer,
occupation or immediate affiliation. It therefore cannot be promoted into the
central research fields without an independent Box 122 bridge and earlier
chronology.

NARA's official companion list for OSS headquarters and headquarters-detachment
records expands the indexed `John L. Chrislow` to `John Laurence Chrislow` in
Roll 23, explicitly identified as part of the Naval Officers Personnel Cards.
The University of Notre Dame Archives' May 1943 *Capstan* volume independently
lists John L. Chrislow of Lodi, Wisconsin, a 1941 University of Wisconsin
graduate, among the second Notre Dame Midshipmen's School class commissioned as
U.S. Naval Reserve ensigns on 27 May 1943. The uncommon exact name, expanded
middle name, naval-officer record series, hometown, school and commissioning
context support a strong identity bridge and a qualified Navy pathway. They do
not name a civilian employer, state the date of OSS assignment, or establish a
formal transfer directly from the school; any imported immediate-affiliation
claim must therefore remain qualified and require the Box 123 file for final
chronology.

The inspected Jean Chrétien chapter is Arthur L. Funk, "The OSS in Algiers," in
George C. Chalou, ed., *The Secrets War* (NARA, 1992), pp. 165-76. On printed
page 167 it identifies OSS cooperation with French intelligence (Service de
Renseignement, or SR), including Col. Jean Chrétien; later text again names
Chrétien in the Algiers liaison context. Combined with the index's `Col` rank
and `French` note, this supports a high-confidence identity and a qualified
French-intelligence military pathway. The chapter does not say that Chrétien
became an OSS employee or supply the formal date and status of the relationship,
so the project must describe cooperation rather than invent a transfer.
