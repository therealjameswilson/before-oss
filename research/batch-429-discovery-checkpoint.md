## Review method

PDF page 84 was rendered at 150 dpi and inspected before research. Every
printed name, initial, rank or blank rank, private identifier or blank, box,
note and location in the ten-row cohort was compared with the immutable
database. Only the two eligible eight-digit identifier shapes were used for
direct comparison to the official Army file. Robert E Clemens's nine-digit,
Geraldine Clement's six-digit and Ira L Clements's nine-character alphanumeric
values were preserved exactly and assessed separately. No value was silently
truncated, padded or converted.

The complete Army file was scanned across 9,200,232 fixed-width records. It
contained one exact-name row for Kent L Clemans, three for Robert E Clemens and
one for Ira L Clements. It contained no exact-name row for Alice B Clem,
Catherine A Clement, Georges Clement, Geraldine Clement, Egidio Clemente,
Marcel J Clemente or Claras B Clements. Exact private-identifier agreement
selected the sole Kent L Clemans row and separately selected an Army row using
the final-letter source variant `Clement Egidio`. None of the Robert E Clemens
or Ira L Clements candidates
matched the differently shaped indexed values. The fixed-width documentation
and rendered occupation-code pages were inspected before selected fields were
interpreted.

Research then proceeded through NARA index context, CIA Reading Room and
archived official-document discovery, exact-name and meaningful variant OSS
searches, employer and occupation searches, institutional finding aids,
obituaries, directories, contemporary newspapers, military-award references
and the current Library of Congress search API. Library of Congress results
were broad discovery counts rather than identity proof: 35 for Alice B Clem,
one for Kent L Clemans, 148 for Robert E Clemens, 120 for Catherine A Clement,
2,895 for Georges Clement, nine for Geraldine Clement, four for Egidio
Clemente, one for Marcel J Clemente, 783 for Claras B Clements and 944 for Ira
L Clements. No Library of Congress item supplied an additional identity-and-
OSS bridge or named pre-OSS employer.

The user-supplied NARA Catalog API credential was not used. No raw Army row,
full private identifier, live API response, home address, phone number,
relative detail or family-sensitive data is retained. Search snippets,
commercial people finders, unsourced genealogy and name-only biographies were
rejected as final evidence. The NYS Historic Newspapers scan was inspected in
its public viewer without solving or bypassing a CAPTCHA. The CIA document was
visually inspected from an archived copy of the former official item because
the current Reading Room item URL now redirects after site decommissioning.

## Source-specific cautions

- The Army merged file is incomplete and is neither an officer register nor a
  Navy file. A non-hit is not negative proof.
- Kent L Clemans's exact identifier selects the sole exact-name Army row. It
  records Army entry on 17 December 1942 and occupation value 681. The rendered
  official code page labels 6-81 `Molders`. The source names no employer,
  foundry, material, exact role or OSS chronology. Its unfamiliar alpha grade
  value remains uninterpreted; enlisted classification rests on the official
  grade coding rather than an invented expansion.
- The three Robert E Clemens Army candidates entered in 1940, 1941 and 1945 and
  carry other identifiers. Their occupations and chronologies are not
  transferred. The index's nine-digit value is neither shortened nor padded.
- Military Times Hall of Valor identifies Georges Clement as a French
  sous-lieutenant attached to OSS for 1944 action near Le Mans and cites
  Headquarters, European Theater of Operations, U.S. Army, General Orders No.
  123 of 16 June 1945. The multi-field match supports high-confidence identity,
  but the secondary index is not a substitute for the cited order or Box 128.
  It does not establish his immediately prior French assignment.
- Egidio Clemente's exact identifier selects an Army row with the source name
  `Clement Egidio`, a 13 May 1943 entry date, birth-year value 1899 and
  occupation value 649. The rendered official code page labels 6-49
  `Occupations in printing and publishing, n.e.c.` The index prints Corporal
  while the Army-entry row records Private; both support enlisted status at
  different moments, without proving a promotion chronology.
- The University of Minnesota's IHRC460 finding aid identifies Egidio Clemente
  as editor and publisher of the Italian-language labor magazine *La Parola
  del Popolo* and then describes World War II U.S. intelligence service in
  Algeria, Sicily and Rome. Rare-name, birth-year, occupation and wartime
  context corroborate the identifier-confirmed identity. The finding aid does
  not supply the employment end date or establish immediate or last-civilian
  status.
- The 5 May 1945 *Nassau Daily Review-Star* states that Marcel J. Clemente was
  formerly employed by a guarantee and trust company in New York, entered the
  Army as a captain in June 1943, served overseas from November 1943 and was
  then with OSS in Italy. The company is not named and his role is not given.
  The evidence supports last civilian employment before Army service, not an
  immediate pre-OSS affiliation.
- CIA FOIA document 00309756, dated 24 May 1945, is an official OSS regimental
  foreign-decorations list naming Captain Marcel J. Clemente. The original item
  page and two-page document were visually checked through the Internet
  Archive's preservation of the former CIA Reading Room URL. The officer number
  is intentionally excluded. This confirms identity and commissioned status;
  it does not identify prewar employment.
- The sole Ira L Clements Army row records a different numeric identifier and
  a 1945 entry date. The later obituary candidate gives the exact name and
  generic veteran status but no branch, identifier or OSS link. Neither is
  merged with the indexed person.
