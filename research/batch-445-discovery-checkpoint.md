## Review method

PDF page 87 was rendered at 150 dpi and visually inspected. Every printed
name, initial, rank, private identifier or blank, box and location in the ten-
row cohort was compared with the immutable database. Henry D Collette and L J
Collier carry eight-digit identifiers; John G Colling carries a literal seven-
digit value. All remain research-only and are masked publicly. The short value
was not padded or altered to force an Army match.

The complete official Army merged file was scanned across 9,200,232 fixed-
width records. Henry D Collette's exact identifier selects the sole exact-name
row, which dates Army entry to 1 February 1943 as a private and carries
civilian-occupation value 736. L J Collier's exact identifier selects an
Enlisted Reserve Corps row named `COLLIER LANGDON J`, dates entry to 22 August
1942 as a private in Signal Corps and carries value 105. The index form and
expanded Langdon form are both retained.

Official NARA technical documentation and visually checked code-list pages
define those values as chauffeurs and drivers, and clerks in general offices.
Both are medium-confidence, strongly date-bounded occupation observations.
Neither names an employer, vehicle, route, office, industry, workplace, later
unit, exact OSS chronology or actual work dates.

The Army file has one exact-name row for John G Colling, dated 8 July 1941 as
a private with civilian-occupation value 048, which NARA defines as draftsmen.
Its identifier differs from the index's later seven-digit officer-format
number. A family-associated chronology independently says Colling joined the
Army in July 1941, became a lieutenant in 1942, requested transfer to the
China-Burma-India theater and was then based in Burma with OSS. An official
U.S. Army history identifies `OSS Captain John G. Colling` with the 1944 Dixie
Mission. The exact name, Captain rank, explicit OSS role and independent entry
month support a high-confidence identity, a qualified draftsman occupation
and a probable-immediate Army affiliation. They do not establish a civilian
employer or exact administrative transfer.

Specialist histories identify Cowles Miles Collier as an OSS member, place him
and his brother Samuel Carnes Collier in the family advertising firm, and
describe their part-time ownership of Motor Sport, Inc. The distinctive name,
explicit OSS statement and adjacent Miles/Sam index rows support Cowles Miles
Collier at high confidence. The family firm is the best-supported last civilian
employer; Motor Sport is separate documented prewar self-employment.

The adjacent `Sam A Collier` row probably represents Samuel Carnes Collier,
but the printed middle initial conflicts and no identifier or personnel-file
bridge resolves it. His family-firm and Motor Sport affiliations therefore
remain explicitly conditional, medium-confidence candidates excluded from
default analytics. The printed A is never replaced by C. Box 135 is required
to confirm or reject the candidate.

The Army file has no exact-name row for Joseph H Collette, Penelope E Colley,
Mildred Collier or Agnes B Collins; it has twenty-three exact-name rows for
Charles A Collins. The file is not a Navy, officer, Marine Corps or
comprehensive OSS roster, so non-hits are not negative proof and none of the
twenty-three Charles candidates is assigned by name alone.

Every person received recorded NARA and CIA context checks, exact-name OSS and
meaningful variant searches, employment and occupation queries, and applicable
institutional, obituary, directory, newspaper, current Library of Congress and
other archival discovery. Modern namesakes, unsourced summaries, people-finder
results and a different Agnes Collins tied to British SOE were rejected. No
authenticated NARA Catalog request was used.
