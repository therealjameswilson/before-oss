## Review method

Every printed name, initial, blank rank, private identifier or blank, box, note
and location in the ten-row cohort was compared with the immutable database.
Four printed identifiers remain research-only and are masked in every public

The complete official Army merged file was scanned across 9,200,232 fixed-
width records. Literal private-identifier comparisons select William J Cole,
George W Cole Jr. and Francis G Coleman. William's identifier distinguishes one
of twenty-nine exact-name Army rows and dates Army entry to 17 February 1943 as
a private, with civilian-occupation value 530. George's identifier selects a
row dated 11 July 1942 as a private; the Army name omits the printed `Jr.` suffix
and carries value 736. Francis's identifier distinguishes one of three exact-
name rows and dates Army entry to 13 February 1943 as a private, with value 992.

The official NARA record-layout and code-list documents were reviewed before
those fields were interpreted. Value 530 is the historical category `Plumbers,
gas fitters, and steam fitters`; value 736 is `Chauffeurs and drivers, bus,
taxi, truck, and tractor`; value 992 is `Students`. The first two are presented
as readable category descriptions without selecting a specific trade or
vehicle. The third is modeled as student status, never employment. All three
remain medium-confidence, strongly date-bounded observations rather than named
employers or demonstrated immediate pre-OSS affiliations.

The Army file produced no exact-name row for Thelma M Cole, Kenneth Colegrove,
Archie F Coleman or Frederick S Coleman. It produced one exact-name row for
Albert M Colegrove, thirteen for Albert Coleman and fifty-two for James E
Coleman. None of those name-only rows was selected. The Army file is incomplete
and is neither an officer, Navy, Marine Corps nor comprehensive OSS roster, so
a non-hit is not negative proof.

Northwestern University Archives identifies Kenneth Wallace Colegrove as a
political scientist who joined Northwestern's faculty in 1919, became a full
professor in 1926, chaired political science from 1940 to 1948, retired in 1952
and served as an OSS consultant from 1943 to 1945. A separate Northwestern
Magazine collection article repeats the faculty, chair and OSS chronology. The
uncommon exact first-and-last-name match, explicit OSS context and absence of a
conflicting index identifier or rank support a high-confidence identity. It
does not reach confirmed because neither institutional source is a direct Box
134 personnel-file linkage.

Northwestern University is published as Colegrove's high-confidence immediate
pre-OSS affiliation and last civilian employer because the documented faculty
appointment began in 1919 and continued through the start of the 1943-1945 OSS
consultancy. The chronology is `strongly_date_bounded`, not
`explicit_immediate`: the sources do not say he left Northwestern, was recruited
from it or had no other consulting work. The site therefore describes a
continuing academic employer that preceded and overlapped OSS service.

Current Library of Congress exact-name discovery returned 43 results for
Thelma M Cole, 16 for Albert M Colegrove, 914 for Albert Coleman, 35 for Archie
F Coleman, 1,904 for Frederick S Coleman and 7,085 for James E Coleman. Ranked
results were reviewed for identity clues, but broad newspaper and book matches
were not treated as evidence. CIA Reading Room searches produced a postwar
Scripps-Howard article by an Albert M Colegrove and a 1944 bibliography citing
Kenneth Colegrove's book; neither was needed to establish a pre-OSS claim. The
Albert reporter, a 1942 Detroit accountant, an earlier Cleveland salesman and a
similar but nonmatching official officer identifier remain rejected candidates
until Box 134 can distinguish them.

Research for every person covered official NARA and CIA context, exact-name OSS
searches and meaningful variants, employment and occupation queries,
institutional archives, obituaries, directories, newspapers, current Library
of Congress discovery and other archival searches. The user-supplied NARA
Catalog API credential was not used. No raw Army row, full private identifier,
live API response, home address, phone number, living-relative detail or
unrelated sensitive data is retained.

## Source-specific cautions

  Corps nor comprehensive OSS register. Non-hits are not negative proof.
- A short printed identifier is not silently padded, truncated or corrected to
  fit a candidate.
- Historical Army-entry occupation categories identify no employer and do not
  establish whether the recorded work immediately preceded OSS service.
- The Army-file omission of George Cole's `Jr.` suffix remains visible; the
  private identifier, not name similarity alone, supports the match.
- `Students` is a relationship category, not an employer, school or degree.
- Northwestern's continuous faculty chronology supports the best-documented
  immediate and last civilian affiliation, but not an explicit recruited-from
  statement or a claim that Colegrove left the university.
- Blank index ranks remain blank. Dated Army-entry private grades are separate
  observations, not rewrites of the index.
