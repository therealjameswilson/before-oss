## Review method

PDF page 84 was rendered at 150 dpi and inspected before research. Every
printed name, initial, blank or stated rank, private identifier or blank, box,
note and location in the ten-row cohort was compared with the immutable
database. The page directly prints `Lt` for Charles K Cloninger and Evelyne
Clopet; it prints no rank for the other eight people. The four printed private
identifiers remain in the research database only and are masked in public
outputs.

The complete official Army merged file was scanned across 9,200,232
fixed-width records. Two identifiers had the exact eight-digit form eligible
for literal comparison. Joseph F Clinton's identifier selects the sole
exact-name row. John L Clive has no exact-name Army row. Edward J Clinton and
Charles K Cloninger have nonstandard-length index values, so no number was
padded or truncated to force a match. Four exact-name Edward rows and one
exact-name Charles row remain rejected candidates. The Charles row is a 1942
private, inconsistent with the index's Lieutenant rank and unsupported by an
identifier bridge. The file has no exact-name row for the remaining six
people. These non-hits are not negative proof: the Army file is incomplete and
is neither an officer, Navy nor Allied register.

Research then proceeded through the NARA index and official context, CIA
Reading Room discovery, exact-name and meaningful variant OSS searches,
employment and occupation searches, institutional archives, obituaries,
directories, newspapers and the current Library of Congress search API.
Library of Congress counts were retained as discovery context only: 761 for
Edward J Clinton, 930 for Joseph F Clinton, 27 for Margery T Clinton, 76 for
John L Clive, 205 for Frank T Cloak, one each for Jan Cloesmeyer, Ida M
Clohan, Elton Clohecy and Evelyne Clopet, and five for Charles K Cloninger.
None supplied a new employer bridge.

John L Clive's existing Harvard institutional chronology was re-audited. It
continues to support high-confidence identification as John Leonard Clive,
born Hans Leo Kleyff, with a United States Army affiliation immediately before
OSS and earlier University of North Carolina student status. The Army-file
non-hit does not overturn this more specific institutional evidence. Neither
the Army assignment nor student status establishes a civilian employer.

The strongest new evidence concerns Evelyne Clopet. A French Ministry of the
Armed Forces profile was downloaded, hashed and visually inspected. It gives
the full name Évelyne Claire Valentine Clopet, the rank `sous-lieutenant`,
volunteer service in the Corps féminin des transmissions, entry into the BCRA
in London in February 1944, integration into Plan Sussex, and radio work under
OSS orders. A Gendarmerie nationale article independently identifies her as a
BCRA radio operator in Sussex. The Service historique de la Défense catalogs
four related file references. Together, the exact rare name, matching officer
rank and explicit OSS context confirm the indexed identity.

The sequence supports the CFT as an earlier Allied military affiliation and
the BCRA as the best-supported affiliation immediately before or accompanying
the OSS-directed assignment. Neither is described as a civilian employer. The
source does not provide a distinct OSS recruitment date or establish whether
BCRA membership continued concurrently, so the immediate relationship is
recorded as strongly date-bounded rather than explicitly immediate. No last
civilian employer is inferred.

A privately compiled Artillery Officer Candidate School roster lists Charles
K Cloninger in class 93 of 1943. The distinctive exact name and officer
training are consistent with the indexed Lieutenant, but the roster provides
no OSS, Box 130 or private-identifier bridge. Charles therefore receives only
a medium-confidence, visibly qualified probable-identity claim. The OCS entry
is not promoted to a pre-OSS affiliation because its relationship to OSS
chronology is unestablished.

The user-supplied NARA Catalog API credential was not used. No raw Army row,
full private identifier, live API response, home address, phone number,
living-relative detail or unrelated sensitive data is retained. Commercial
people finders, unsourced genealogy, search snippets and name-only biographies
were rejected as final evidence. No access restriction, paywall or CAPTCHA was
bypassed.

## Source-specific cautions

- Joseph F Clinton's exact private identifier selects a 5 December 1942 Army
  entry as a private. Occupation value 065 is decoded from the visually
  inspected official code page as `Embalmers and undertakers`. This broad
  historical category names no funeral home, employer, municipality or exact
  role.
- Edward J Clinton's seven-digit value and Charles K Cloninger's six-digit
  value are preserved exactly in the private database. Neither is normalized
  to an Army-file identifier. Literal comparison therefore selects no row.
- The Army merged file is incomplete and is not a comprehensive officer,
  Navy, Allied or OSS register. John L Clive's non-hit and the other non-hits
  do not disprove service or identity.
- Jan Wimpi Cloesmeyer, Jan D Cloesmeyer and a differently spelled RAF
  casualty remain unselected alternatives. A name resemblance is not enough
  to transfer nationality, occupation or service history.
- Elton Clohecy's rare-name oral-history and cemetery candidates are postwar
  leads only. Neither supplies an OSS or prewar identity bridge.
- The Artillery OCS roster is a modern private compilation and is rated as
  corroborative. It supports probable identity only, not an employer or
  immediate affiliation.
- The French official evidence establishes CFT, BCRA and OSS-directed Sussex
  service. It does not identify Evelyne Clopet's own civilian employer. A
  relative's employer is irrelevant and was not projected onto her.
- The SHD catalog lists files but does not show that they are digitized or that
  this project has reviewed their contents.
