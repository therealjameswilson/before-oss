## Review method

PDF page 85 was rendered at 150 dpi and visually inspected before research.
Every printed name, initial, rank or blank, private identifier or blank, box,
note and location in the ten-row cohort was compared with the immutable
database. The index prints `Sgt` for Donald E Cobleigh and no rank for the
other nine people. Five private identifiers remain research-only and are
masked in every public output. `Major` is the printed first name in the Major B
Cochran row; it was not converted into a military rank.

The complete official Army merged file was scanned across 9,200,232
fixed-width records. Literal comparisons tested all five eligible printed
identifiers without silently padding, truncating or otherwise rewriting them.
Angelo Coccoli has one exact identifier-and-name match. Donald E Cobleigh,
Gwynne W Coburn, Major B Cochran and Lyle N Cockerille have no identifier
match. The complete name review found no exact-name row for those four people,
Sarah E Coburn, Elizabeth Cochran, Jefferson Cochran or Barbara L Cochrane.
One exact-name John M Cochran row was found, but the index supplies no
identifier, rank, residence, birth year or second corroborating fact; the row
remains an unselected name-only candidate.

Official NARA record-layout and code-list documents were reviewed before any
Army field was interpreted. Angelo's identifier-backed row dates Army entry to
6 July 1942, records a private enlisted grade and occupation value 768. The
rendered official code page labels entry 7-68 `Packing, filling, labeling,
marking, bottling, and related occupations, n. e. c.` That residual category
names no product, company, plant, workplace or exact role and does not
establish when the work occurred relative to OSS service.

Donald E Cobleigh produced the batch's one verified employer pathway. A Wilkes
College institutional newspaper published in 1948 explicitly identifies the
rare exact name as a former Dartmouth instructor who served with OSS as a
member of the United States Marine Corps. Contemporary Dartmouth sources
independently show Donald E. Cobleigh's promotion from music instructor to
assistant professor in 1935, identify Professor Cobleigh's music work in
December 1942, list him among faculty and staff who had left for war service by
June 1943, and place him in the Marine Corps in an August 1943 war directory.
A Dartmouth retrospective supplies the longer faculty chronology: assistant
in music in 1925, instructor the next year, assistant professor in 1935, and
Glee Club director.

The rare exact name and middle initial, indexed Sergeant rank, explicit
OSS-and-Marine statement and continuous institutional chronology support a
high-confidence identity and enlisted Marine Corps classification. They do not
provide a private identifier or direct personnel-file linkage, so the identity
is not promoted to confirmed. Dartmouth College is published at high
confidence as the last documented civilian employer before wartime service.
It is not labeled the immediate pre-OSS affiliation because the accessible
sources do not establish whether Marine Corps service intervened before the
OSS assignment or was concurrent with it.

Research for every person also covered official NARA and CIA context,
exact-name OSS searches and meaningful variants, employment and occupation
queries, institutional archives, obituaries, directories, newspapers, current
Library of Congress discovery and other archival searches. The Dartmouth
manuscript catalog presented a CAPTCHA to the generic client; no restriction
was bypassed and its search metadata was not used as substantive evidence.
Modern people-finder material, unsourced genealogy, search snippets and
name-only biographies were rejected as final evidence. Sensitive modern
details were not retained.

The user-supplied NARA Catalog API credential was not used. No raw Army row,
full private identifier, live API response, home address, phone number,
living-relative detail or unrelated sensitive data is retained.
