## Review method

PDF page 86 was rendered at 150 dpi and visually inspected before research.
Every printed name, initial, blank rank, private identifier or blank, box, note
and location in the ten-row cohort was compared with the immutable database.
The printed `Leoonard` spelling remains the public indexed form. Six printed
identifiers, including the two six-digit forms for John H Colby and William E
Colby, remain research-only and are masked in every public output.

The complete official Army merged file was scanned across 9,200,232
fixed-width records. Literal comparisons tested the printed identifiers
without padding, truncating or rewriting them. Four standard eight-digit
identifiers select Army rows: the indexed Leoonard A Colavita selects `COLAVITA
LEONARD A`; Calvin Colding selects `COLDING CALVIN H`; Charles H Cole Jr.
selects `COLES CHARLES H JR`; and Francis J Cole selects one of five exact-name
Army rows. The exact identifier is decisive in each case, while every spelling
or middle-initial difference remains visible rather than silently normalized.

John H Colby has one exact-name Army row, but its standard identifier differs
from the printed six-digit value. William E Colby has two exact-name Army rows,
and neither standard identifier matches the printed six-digit value. Neither
short value was padded or otherwise converted to select a candidate. Frank
Cole has ten exact-name Army rows with no index identifier to distinguish them.
John A Colborn, Anita B Cole and Betina Cole have no exact-name Army row. These
non-hits are not negative proof because the Army file is incomplete and is not
a comprehensive military or OSS roster.

Official NARA record-layout and code-list documents were reviewed before Army
fields were interpreted. Leonard entered the Army as a private on 3 July 1943;
Charles as a private in the Enlisted Reserve Corps on 3 October 1942; and
Francis with private grade code 8 in the Enlisted Reserve Corps on 15 December
1942. Each record carries civilian-occupation value 992, which the official
table defines as `Students`. Student status is modeled as `student`, never as
employment, and no school, course or employer is inferred.

Calvin entered the Army as a private on 7 May 1943. His record carries value
736, which the official table defines broadly as `Chauffeurs and drivers, bus,
taxi, truck, and tractor`. The complete category is retained rather than
selecting a vehicle or job. It identifies no employer, client, route, industry
or workplace. All four observations are medium-confidence, date-bounded
occupation/status evidence and are excluded from employer analytics.

A 1943 Office of Price Administration manual, preserved by the Government
Publishing Office, lists `John A. Colborn, Jr.` as the federal mileage
administrator for the Office of Strategic Services. The printed page was
visually inspected at 200 dpi. Exact first name, surname and middle initial plus
contemporaneous OSS context support a probable match to the identifier-free
Box 133 row. The source alone does not supply the second independent identifier
required for a high-confidence identity, however, and it documents an OSS
administrative role rather than a pre-OSS affiliation. The result is therefore
published only as a qualified identity claim.

William E Colby's existing high-confidence identity and high-confidence
immediate U.S. Army affiliation remain unchanged. His terminal status is
corrected from `verified_employer_found` to `requires_archival_review` because
a military assignment is not an employer and no last civilian employer has
been verified. This decreases the status bucket by one without changing the
verified-employer or verified-affiliation coverage metrics.

John H Colby, Anita B Cole, Betina Cole and Frank Cole remain unresolved. No
candidate source supplied the corroborating identifier, Box linkage, wartime
chronology or direct personnel-file connection needed to select one identity.
Modern people finders and living-person contact data were excluded. Cemetery,
genealogy, patent, academic, directory and same-name results were rejected
when they lacked an OSS or archival bridge.

Research for every person covered official NARA and CIA context, exact-name
OSS searches and meaningful variants, employment and occupation queries,
institutional archives, obituaries, directories, newspapers, current Library
of Congress discovery and other archival searches. Search snippets, unsourced
genealogy, modern people finders, relative-centered material and name-only
biographies were rejected as final evidence. Sensitive modern details were not
retained.

The user-supplied NARA Catalog API credential was not used. No raw Army row,
full private identifier, live API response, home address, phone number,
living-relative detail or unrelated sensitive data is retained.

## Source-specific cautions

- The Army merged file is incomplete and is neither an officer, Navy, Marine
  Corps nor comprehensive OSS register. Non-hits are not negative proof.
- A short printed identifier is not silently padded, truncated or assigned to
  an identifier system merely to select a candidate.
- Historical Army-entry occupation categories identify no employer and do not
  establish whether the recorded status immediately preceded OSS service.
- Student status is distinct from employment; no unnamed school is modeled as
  an employer or organization.
- The Army file's Leonard/Leoonard, Colding/Calvin H and Cole/Coles differences
  are documented variants supported by exact private identifiers, not silent
  corrections to the NARA index.
- The 1943 mileage directory supports only a probable John A Colborn identity
  and an OSS-era role. It does not establish a pre-OSS employer.
- William E Colby's immediate Army affiliation remains distinct from the
  unresolved question of his last civilian employer before service.
- Blank index ranks remain blank; dated Army-entry private grades are separate
  observations and are not presented as later OSS ranks.
