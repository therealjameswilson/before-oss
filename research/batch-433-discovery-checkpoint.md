## Review method

PDF pages 84 and 85 were rendered at 150 dpi and inspected before research.
Every printed name, initial, blank rank, private identifier or blank, box, note
and location in the ten-row cohort was compared with the immutable database.
The index prints no rank for any person in this batch. The three private
identifiers remain research-only and are masked in every public output.

The complete official Army merged file was scanned across 9,200,232
fixed-width records. Lawrence W Cloutier, Wilbert A Clower and William
Clugston each have an eligible eight-digit index identifier, and each literal
identifier comparison selected one Army row. Lawrence and Wilbert match the
indexed names exactly. William's selected row adds middle initial E to the
index's blank middle-initial field. A unique exact-name Bartine H Coady row
was separately adjudicated without an index identifier. No exact-name Army row
was found for the other six people. These non-hits are not negative proof: the
Army file is incomplete and is neither an officer, Navy nor comprehensive OSS
register.

Official NARA record-layout and code-list documents were reviewed before any
Army field was interpreted. Lawrence's broad occupation value 999 remains
uninterpreted. Wilbert's Enlisted Reserve Corps layout decodes the relevant
value as `Students`. William's standard occupation value decodes as `Mechanics
and repairmen, n.e.c.` Bartine's unique row also decodes to `Students`. The
William and Bartine grade characters are malformed and were not normalized.
Student status is not employment, and neither broad category supplies a school,
employer, specialty or work location.

Research then proceeded through the NARA index and official context, CIA
Reading Room discovery, exact-name and meaningful variant OSS searches,
employment and occupation searches, institutional archives, obituaries,
directories, newspapers, current Library of Congress discovery and other
archival searches. Common-name and later-life results were not assigned to an
index entry without corroborating identifiers. A 2011 obituary guestbook lead
for a Helen Coakley mentions seeking work in Chicago in 1942, but it supplies
no middle initial, OSS reference, private identifier or Box 130 bridge and was
rejected.

The strongest civilian-employment evidence concerns Rhea G Clyman. A
fourteen-page, image-only scholarly profile from the Canadian Institute of
Ukrainian Studies was OCRed only for discovery. Physical pages 1, 3, 4 and 13
were rendered at 150 dpi and visually inspected before claims were retained.
It identifies Rhea Clyman as Rachel Gertrude Clyman, says Walter Duranty hired
her as his secretary and assistant after she arrived in Moscow in December
1928, describes her subsequent independent freelance reporting, and calls her
later Munich correspondence for the Daily Telegraph a job. It also describes
later work as the paper's Canadian correspondent and a move to the United
States in 1942.

The distinctive exact name and full middle name support a high-confidence
identity match, but no private identifier or explicit Box 130 linkage is
available, so the identity is not labeled confirmed. The three work
relationships are modeled separately: employment by the named hiring party,
self-employment as a freelance journalist, and employment by the Daily
Telegraph. The New York Times is not silently assigned as Clyman's employer
merely because Duranty was its correspondent, and newspapers that bought
freelance stories are not converted into employers. All three relationships
are documented prewar affiliations only. The accessible chronology does not
prove which was immediately before OSS or the last civilian role before
wartime service.

An official U.S. Army Special Operations history identifies Bartine H Coady
as a former OSS member and explicitly dates recruitment to August 1945 in
Washington, D.C. The rare exact name, middle initial, OSS context and unique
Army merged-file row support a high-confidence identity. The article's `1LT`
label appears in a table about later Special Forces service and is not
projected backward into OSS-era rank or index classification. Neither source
identifies Coady's school, civilian employer or pre-OSS Army unit.

The user-supplied NARA Catalog API credential was not used. No raw Army row,
full private identifier, live API response, home address, phone number,
living-relative detail or unrelated sensitive data is retained. Commercial
people finders, unsourced genealogy, search snippets and name-only biographies
were rejected as final evidence. No access restriction, paywall or CAPTCHA was
bypassed.

## Source-specific cautions

- An Army merged-file match establishes only what its selected fields support.
  It is not proof of an OSS assignment date, named employer or immediate
  predecessor.
- Lawrence W Cloutier's value 999 is left uninterpreted rather than guessed.
- Wilbert A Clower's and Bartine H Coady's student categories name no school
  and are never counted as employers.
- William E Clugston's broad mechanic category names no employer, specialty or
  workplace. His malformed grade is not used for classification.
- Walter Duranty is the named hiring party in the Clyman source. His own New
  York Times relationship is not transferred to Clyman.
- Rhea Clyman's Daily Telegraph job is definite prewar employment but is not
  established as immediate pre-OSS or her last civilian employer before
  service.
- Bartine H Coady's postwar Special Forces lieutenant label is not treated as
  an OSS-era rank. The index itself prints no rank.
- The Helen Coakley obituary lead remains rejected; no biographical or
  employment detail is transferred to Helen I Coakley.
