## Reviewed outcomes

- A 1939 Virginia Military Institute former-cadet register identifies William
  Gerhard Suhling Jr. as president of Suhling & Co., Inc., a Lynchburg
  leaf-tobacco packing, dealing and exporting company. Official CIA historical
  records corroborate his OSS-era name and lieutenant-colonel role. The company
  is published as qualified earlier pre-OSS employment, not as the immediate
  affiliation or last civilian employer because no separation date is stated.
- A Japanese American National Museum historical article identifies Yaemitsu
  Sugimachi as a Rafu Shimpo reporter connected to a 1932 Los Angeles radio
  broadcast. A City of Albuquerque museum roster records his 1942 occupation
  as teacher but names no school or employer. Both are published with a
  probable identity assessment; neither is treated as proof of an OSS role.
- A NARA-derived enemy-alien case-file list supports Mitsue Marie Sugimachi as
  a probable name expansion for the indexed Mitsue M. Sugimachi. It supplies
  no employer and is not used to infer a relationship to Yaemitsu or an OSS
  function.
- A French War Ministry certificate establishes Jacques Suissa's 1944 service
  in the Sussex (Proust) network and a mission-duration corresponding grade of
  sous-lieutenant. It supports foreign/Allied commissioned classification but
  no pre-OSS employer.
- Visual inspection of the Field Artillery OCS roster resolves Orman Suker as
  class 72-43, which ran from 15 April through 7 July 1943. A scholarly study
  later identifies him as an OSS lieutenant in Bari by 6 October 1944. OCS is
  published as prior military training, not civilian employment or a proven
  immediate transfer source.
- Five accepted Army records provide dated historical occupation or status
  categories: drivers, dairy farmers, statistical clerks and compilers, and
  students. None names an employer, farm, school or workplace.
- A David Sugimoto/Agar Company lead and common-name Sullivan biographies
