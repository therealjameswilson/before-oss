## Army merged-file adjudication

The complete official Army merged file was scanned across all 9,200,232
fixed-width records. It produced exact private-identifier and exact-name
matches for Ismael Cordero, Victor Cordovi, Donald L Coriell and John W Corley.
No literal identifier is retained in this checkpoint or the public evidence.

- Ismael Cordero entered the Army on 2 March 1942 as a private. The statistical
  card carries civilian-occupation value `229`; the official table defines
  `2-29` as `Kitchen workers in hotels, restaurants, railroads, steamships,
  etc., n.e.c.` This is a broad Army-entry observation, not an employer or proof
  of sequence relative to OSS.
- Victor Cordovi entered the Army on 9 October 1942 as a private. The card's
  `073` value means `Wholesale managers`, but the independent memorial places
  his OSS volunteering after arrival in January 1941. Because the Army entry
  postdates that reported OSS start and the sources disagree about his arrival
  year, the occupation value is not published as pre-OSS evidence.
- Donald L Coriell entered the Army on 26 March 1943 as a private. Value `861`
  is the unskilled level of `Occupations in manufacture of boots and shoes`.
  It identifies neither a task nor an employer and does not prove sequence
  relative to OSS.
- John W Corley's identifier-matched record is dated 27 October 1945, after
  OSS dissolution, and carries a master-sergeant grade. It confirms the Army
  identity and enlisted classification but is not evidence of a pre-OSS Army
  assignment or civilian occupation. Seven other exact-name Army rows belong
  to different identifiers and remain separate.

The merged file has no exact-name row for Robert Cordes, Frances A Cordle or

## Public-source adjudication

The CIA adapter ran one bounded exact-name query per person and returned no
candidates. The Library of Congress adapter ran one bounded employment-focused
query per person. Nine returned no candidates. Robert Cordes produced an 1873
newspaper page whose OCR merely places the separate words `Roberts` and

Exact-name OSS, employer, occupation, obituary, institutional, newspaper and
archival web searches were reviewed for all ten people. Common-name, genealogy,
people-finder, wrong-initial, wrong-surname and postwar-only results lack the
Box 144 or identifier bridge and are not used. A Portsmouth directory's bare
Donald Coriell residence line, a WWII Robert E Cordes roster, and the
`Cordell`-spelled Frances directory result are examples of rejected leads.

HerbalGram 32's trade-publication memorial identifies Victor A Cordovi as born
in Sofia in 1911, arriving in New York in January 1941, and joining OSS after a
short time in the United States. The memorial says he served in Cairo and
Italy and was discharged from OSS in November 1945. NARA's official Donovan
microfilm roll list independently lists a 23 October 1944 `Cable-Victor
Cordovi` under OSS Operations - Balkans. Both pages were rendered and visually
inspected. The rare exact name, matching 1911 Army birth-year value, exact
private-identifier Army record and two explicit OSS sources confirm the
indexed identity.

International Botanical Ingredients' company history independently repeats
Cordovi's OSS volunteering but says he arrived in the United States in 1943,
not 1941. The disagreement is preserved. Neither account names a pre-OSS
employer; both place the spice career after OSS. No postwar spice company is
recast as a pre-OSS employer.
