## Review method

PDF page 84 was rendered at 150 dpi and inspected before research. Every
printed name, initial, blank rank, private identifier or blank, box, blank note
and location in the ten-row cohort was compared with the immutable database.
Only three eligible private identifier shapes were used for direct comparison
to the official Army file. Edmund C Cleveland's five-digit value was preserved
exactly and was not silently treated as an officer number or padded into a
service number.

The complete Army merged file was scanned across 9,200,232 fixed-width
records. It contained four exact-name Joseph E Clements rows, one Harold V
Cleveland row and one David H Clift row. It contained no exact-name row for
Ysobel Clements, Edmund C Cleveland, Eleanor E Cleveland, Margaret L Cleveland,
Zito Cleveland, Acice C Clifford or Elizabeth A Clifford. Exact private-
identifier agreement selected Harold V Cleveland and David H Clift. A third
punctuation-normalized identifier selected Elizabeth A Clifford's fixed-width
Women's Army Corps record, whose name field is compressed in the source. No
Joseph E Clements row could be selected. The official layout documentation and
rendered occupation-code page were inspected before any selected field was
interpreted.

Research then proceeded through NARA index context, CIA Reading Room and
official-document discovery, exact-name and meaningful variant OSS searches,
employment and occupation searches, institutional archives, obituaries,
directories, newspapers, the 1946 Official Army Register and the current
Library of Congress search API. Library of Congress results were broad
discovery counts rather than identity proof: 1,225 for Joseph E Clements, 23
for Ysobel Clements, 174 for Edmund C Cleveland, 72 for Eleanor E Cleveland,
284 for Harold V Cleveland, 291 for Margaret L Cleveland, one for Zito
Cleveland, one for Acice C Clifford, 239 for Elizabeth A Clifford and 32 for
David H Clift. No Library of Congress item supplied an additional pre-OSS
employer claim.

The user-supplied NARA Catalog API credential was not used. No raw Army row,
full private identifier, live API response, home address, phone number,
relative detail or family-sensitive data is retained. Search snippets,
commercial people finders, unsourced genealogy and name-only biographies were
rejected as final evidence. Official PDFs and code pages were rendered and
visually checked; no access restriction or CAPTCHA was bypassed.

## Source-specific cautions

- The Army merged file is incomplete and is neither an officer register nor a
  Navy file. A non-hit is not negative proof.
- None of four exact-name Joseph E Clements Army rows can be selected without
  a source identifier. Name-only 63rd Division and 93rd Armored Field Artillery
  candidates likewise lack an OSS or Box 129 bridge. No occupation, birthplace,
  unit or entry chronology is transferred.
- The five-digit value printed for Edmund C Cleveland matches no exact-name
  Army row. Printed pages 128-129 of the 1946 Official Army Register contain
  Cleveland entries but not Edmund C Cleveland. This bounded negative check
  neither identifies the value nor proves that the person did not serve.
- Harold V Cleveland's identifier selects the sole exact-name Army row, an
  entry dated 26 December 1944 as a private with occupation value 081. The
  rendered official code page labels 0-81 `Advertising agents`. It names no
  agency, employer, client or precise role.
- A 1 November 1945 War Department Strategic Services Unit dispatch names
  Margaret Cleveland and Elizabeth Clifford among vouchered civilians being
  transferred to Nuremberg war-crimes work. For Margaret, exact name and SSU
  context support only a probable match because the initial and identifier are
  absent. For Elizabeth, the dispatch corroborates later context after the
  identifier-confirmed Army record. In neither case is late-1945 civilian
  status treated as prewar employment or silently made the index-time category.
- The Elizabeth A Clifford identifier selects a source row whose fixed-width
  name is compressed, with a 23 February 1943 entry date, auxiliary grade, WAC
  branch and occupation value 104. Official code 1-04 is `Clerks, general`.
  The category does not identify an office or employer.
- David H Clift's identifier selects the sole exact-name Army row, with a 7
  August 1942 entry date as a private and occupation value 023. Official code
  0-23 is `Librarians`. The category corroborates, but does not replace, the
  separately sourced named-employer chronology.
- The American Library Association Archives record states that Clift began
  reference work at the New York Public Library after his 1931 library-science
  degree, became assistant to the Columbia University Library director in
  1937, and after five years was drafted in 1942. It then explicitly sequences
  an Army hospital-orderly assignment before OSS. Columbia is therefore the
  strongly date-bounded last civilian employer, the Army is the explicit
  immediate affiliation, and New York Public Library is earlier documented
  prewar employment. Columbia employment remains distinct from his education
  at the Columbia University School of Library Service.
- Postwar Yale, ALA and Library of Congress mission work is not used as
  pre-OSS evidence.
