## Review method

PDF page 86 was rendered at 150 dpi and visually inspected before research.
Every printed name, initial, suffix, blank rank, private identifier or blank,
box, note and location in the ten-row cohort was compared with the immutable
database. Nine rows print Box 133 and Edwin J Cohn Jr. prints Box 132; all ten
print location `230/86/28/07` and no rank. Four printed identifiers remain
research-only and are masked in every public output. Repeated Cohen and Cohn
surnames are not treated as evidence of kinship, duplication or a shared
biography.

The complete official Army merged file was scanned across 9,200,232
fixed-width records. Literal comparisons tested all four printed identifiers
without silently padding, truncating or rewriting them. Noah J Cohen, Sidney L
Cohen and Carl M Cohn each have one exact identifier-and-name match. Louise D
Cohen's six-digit value does not select an exact-name row; the literal sequence
appears in unrelated records only as a substring or within a different
prefixed identifier. Exact-name candidate counts were reviewed for all ten
people. Five Robert A Cohen rows, five Sidney L Cohen rows and fourteen
Theodore Cohen rows were not selected without the required identifier or
corroborating index bridge.

Official NARA record-layout and code-list documents were reviewed before any
Army field was interpreted. Noah entered the Army as a private on 3 June 1941,
Sidney as a corporal on 24 October 1942, and Carl as a private on 3 November
1942. Their decoded civilian-occupation categories are `Sales clerks`, `Food
products producing occupations, miscellaneous`, and `Foremen, n.e.c.` These
historical categories identify neither an employer nor an exact workplace,
product, industry or job. All three are published at medium confidence with
uncertain relation to later OSS service.

The 1948 official OSS Assessment Staff roster provides a separate pathway for
Mabel B. Cohen and Robert A. Cohen. Its introductory key says the institution
in parentheses is the one with which a staff member was officially connected
before government service; the rank shown is the highest attained while
working for the OSS Schools and Training Branch. The roster pairs Mabel B.
Cohen with Chestnut Lodge Sanitarium and Robert A. Cohen with Sheppard-Pratt
Hospital. Robert's institutional oral history says that he and his wife both
entered psychiatry and had worked at Chestnut Lodge. A Washington Post obituary
identifies his first wife as Dr. Mabel Blake Cohen, says Robert began work as a
physician at Sheppard and Enoch Pratt Hospital, and places Navy Medical Corps
service afterward.

That evidence supports qualified employer pathways, but it does not erase the
index-side identity limits. Mabel's row has no middle initial, rank or
identifier. Robert's common-name row has a middle initial but no rank or
identifier. Both identities remain `probable`, both employer claims remain
medium confidence, and both are excluded from default high-confidence
analytics. Chestnut Lodge is Mabel's best-supported immediate affiliation and
last civilian employer. Sheppard and Enoch Pratt is Robert's best-supported
immediate affiliation and last civilian employer. Robert's Commander (USNR)
grade is retained in the identity evidence but is not applied as settled index
classification until Box 133 confirms the match. The historical institutional
name forms are preserved separately from any modern branding.

UCLA Library Special Collections supplies a direct OSS chronology for a
Theodore Cohen born in New York City in 1918: he graduated from City College in
1938, taught history there in 1939-1940, and then worked for OSS military
intelligence from 1941 to 1944. Teaching is modeled as employment rather than
student status. The narrative sequence supports City College as the best-
supported immediate affiliation and last civilian employer with
`strongly_date_bounded` temporal evidence. The index row has no middle initial,
rank, identifier or role, so the identity and employer claims remain probable
and medium confidence rather than settled facts.

The Marcel Cohen row produced a plausible but unconfirmed filmmaker pathway.
The Bibliothèque nationale de France identifies `Cohen, Marcel (1908-2002)` as
a rejected name form for French director Marcel Cravenne. AFI credits Marcel
Cohen as film editor on the 1943 U.S. War Department film *The Battle of
Russia*. Cineteca di Bologna identifies Marcel Cohen as Marcel Cravenne in the
1944 credits for *Salut à la France* and notes Office of Strategic Services
collaboration on that film. The exact name, linked alias and wartime OSS context
support a probable identity and qualified occupation lead. They do not provide
a personnel-file identifier, direct service statement, employer or OSS entry
date. The film-editor and director occupation therefore remains
`temporal_relation_uncertain`, never an immediate affiliation or employer.

Louise D Cohen remains ambiguous after a second staged protocol pass. The
existing Area B Louis D Cohen and wartime Lewis spelling remain a low-
confidence, withheld candidate only; visual review and the literal identifier
test supplied no bridge. Sarah Cohen and Edwin J Cohn Jr. remain unresolved.
The exact-name Sarah Army candidates lack an index-side identifier. Edwin L,
R and M Cohn Army candidates have incompatible initials, and the prominent
biochemist Edwin J. Cohn lacks the printed Jr. suffix and any reviewed OSS
bridge. None is promoted to the index entity.

Research for every person also covered official NARA and CIA context,
exact-name OSS searches and meaningful variants, employment and occupation
queries, institutional archives, obituaries, directories, newspapers, current
Library of Congress discovery and other archival searches. Search snippets,
unsourced genealogy, modern people finders, relative-centered material and
name-only biographies were rejected as final evidence. Sensitive modern
details were not retained.

The user-supplied NARA Catalog API credential was not used. No raw Army row,
full private identifier, live API response, home address, phone number,
living-relative detail or unrelated sensitive data is retained.

## Source-specific cautions

- The Army merged file is incomplete and is neither an officer, Navy, Marine
  Corps nor comprehensive OSS register. Non-hits are not negative proof.
- Historical Army-entry occupation categories identify no employer and do not
  establish whether the recorded status immediately preceded OSS service.
- The official Assessment Staff roster establishes pre-government
  institutions for its named staff, but the identifier-free index rows still
  require Box 133 confirmation.
- The Washington Post provides employer and Navy chronology for Robert and
  identifies Mabel Blake Cohen as his first wife; it does not itself link
  either person to the page-86 index rows.
- Theodore Cohen's UCLA chronology is direct and institutional, but a common
  name plus OSS context alone does not satisfy the project's high-confidence
  identity threshold.
- Marcel Cohen's film credits overlap wartime service and do not establish an
  immediate pre-OSS employer or sequence.
- A printed identifier is never silently altered to select a namesake.
- Shared surnames or adjacent index rows do not by themselves establish family
  relationships or duplicate people.
