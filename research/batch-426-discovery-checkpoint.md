## Review method

PDF page 83 was rendered at 150 dpi and inspected before research. Every
printed name, initial, rank or blank, private identifier or blank, box and
location in the ten-row cohort was compared with the immutable database. Only
the two eight-digit values eligible for comparison were submitted to a complete
transient scan of the 9,200,232-row Army Serial Number Merged File. The two
seven-digit values were not padded or treated as Army identifiers. Exact-name
Army candidates were counted independently for all ten people. The fixed-width
documentation and rendered NARA code-list pages for the two selected records
were inspected before any field was interpreted.

Research proceeded through exact-name and variant OSS searches, CIA Reading
Room discovery, employer and occupation searches, institutional, obituary,
newspaper and archival searches, and the current Library of Congress search
API. The Library of Congress queries behaved as broad token discovery rather
than reliable exact-name retrieval: they returned 68 results for Kathleen, 17
for Marianne, 197 for Mildred, 106 for Norman, 456 for Paul, 53 for Ruby,
10,557 for William, zero for Harold, nine for Henri and zero for Frederick. One
Mildred request timed out and succeeded on a bounded retry. No returned item
supplied the identity and OSS bridge needed to select a person.

The user-supplied NARA Catalog API credential was not used. No raw Army row,
full private identifier, live API response, home address, phone number,
relative detail or family-sensitive data is retained here. Search snippets,
commercial people finders, unsourced genealogy and name-only biographies were
rejected as final evidence.

## Source-specific cautions

- The Army merged file is incomplete and is not a Navy file. A non-hit is not
  negative proof.
- Norman F Clarke's exact identifier and name select one official row, dated 3
  June 1942 and carrying Private. Its Enlisted Reserve Corps occupation value
  017 is labeled on the rendered official code page as Engineers, electrical.
  The record's branch values are not expanded because their meaning was not
  established in the reviewed documentation.
- William W Clarke's exact identifier and name select one official row, dated
  29 June 1944 and carrying Private. Its standard-card occupation value 992 is
  labeled on the rendered official code page as Students. Student status is not
  employment; no school or immediate OSS predecessor is named.
- An official CIA Reading Room discovery result says a Marianne Clarke reported
  as a Special Funds Branch secretary on 23 August 1944. The direct PDF
  redirected to the collection landing page during review, leaving the page and
  full context uninspected. The lead is therefore withheld from public facts.
  A Marianne Clarke listed in a 1939-1940 Nebraska educational directory cannot
  be joined to that secretary by name alone.
- Paul W Clarke's printed S 2/c rank is direct evidence of enlisted naval status.
  The sole exact-name Army row carries another identifier and is rejected. A
  1931 Congressional Record list names a Paul W. Clarke among midshipmen
  appointed ensign, but the government PDF was temporarily unavailable and no
  index or OSS bridge connects the namesake.
- Harold W Classen's exact-name Army row remains a private lead. The seven-digit
  index value is preserved without padding. A Schertz municipal veterans list
  describes an exact-name SP4 serving in 1953-1955 and is chronologically
  incompatible with wartime OSS service.
- A published scholarly footnote points to a 10 February 1941 Henri L. Claudel
  letter in FDR Library President's Personal File 5847. It is an archival lead,
  not proof that the correspondent is the indexed person. The better-known
  French general and several Claudel-family namesakes do not supply the needed
  bridge.
- A discovery-only genealogy result gives a Frederick Claudy a 1912-1997 life
  span, and a 1964 affidavit was notarized by Frederick W. Claudy Jr. Neither
  supplies a wartime identifier, occupation or OSS evidence. Results for author
  Carl H. Claudy are different people.
