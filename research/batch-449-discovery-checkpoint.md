## Review method

PDF page 88 was rendered at 150 dpi and visually inspected. Every printed name,
initial, rank or blank, private identifier or blank, box, note and location in
the ten-row cohort was compared with the immutable database. Howard G Comfort
carries a seven-digit printed value. It remains literal and was not padded or
treated as an eight-digit Army identifier. Mattiie H Commodore's double-i given
name was confirmed against the page image and remains unchanged; `Mattie` was
used only as a documented search alias. Full private identifiers remain
research-only and are masked publicly.

The complete official Army merged file was scanned across all 9,200,232
fixed-width records. Its compressed source was previously verified at
185,043,578 bytes with SHA-256
`80c46e83937c6483d11def47fbb1629cab0ac135694fdfa09268b66d826f1066`.
Exact private-identifier matches were found for Albert R Combs, Joseph F
Comerford and Adolph Commodaro. Exact-name candidates were reviewed for every
cohort member. George I Combs has an exact-middle-initial Army candidate dated
29 November 1946, but no identifier or OSS bridge assigns it to the indexed
person. Howard G Comfort has an exact-name Army candidate with a different
identifier; it likewise remains unassigned. The Army file is not an officer,
Navy, Marine Corps, women's-service, foreign-personnel or comprehensive OSS
roster, so non-hits are not negative proof.

Albert R Combs's exact match records an Army entry event on 1 February 1946,
after NARA's documented termination of OSS on 20 September 1945. It supports a
high-confidence identity and enlisted Army classification but cannot establish
a pre-OSS occupation or pathway. Its occupation value 000 has no label in the
inspected official code list and was not decoded. The chronology is routed to
critical temporal review.

Joseph F Comerford's exact match records Army entry on 20 February 1943 as a
private with civilian-occupation value 002. NARA's official code list defines
that historical category as `Actors and actresses`. A separate NARA OSS
European-theater awards roster dated 1 March 1945 carries the same exact name
and private identifier, identifies him as a sergeant in the 31st SCI Unit and
notes Normandy service. The two official identifier-bearing sources confirm
identity. The occupation category is published with medium confidence and does
not name an employer, production, medium, workplace or later Army-to-OSS
sequence.

Adolph Commodaro's exact match records Army entry on 10 May 1943 as a private
with civilian-occupation value 426. The official code list defines the category
as `Tailors and tailoresses`. This confirms identity and enlisted Army status,
but the occupation label names no employer, shop, specialty, workplace or
Army-to-OSS chronology. It is published only as a qualified occupation claim.

A NARA OSS awards roster dated 11 December 1944 names First Lieutenant Howard
G. Comfort under the Field Research and Reports Detachment and notes Northern
France and Germany campaign participation. Its officer identifier contains the
same digits as the literal seven-digit index value but in a transposed order.
The exact uncommon name and direct OSS context support a high-confidence match
and commissioned Army classification, while the unresolved discrepancy
prevents confirmation. Neither private number is published or silently
repaired; Box 136 is required to adjudicate them.

A San Francisco Chronicle obituary reproduced by Kythera-family.net identifies
Nicholas H. Cominos by the exact rare name, says that he worked at the Cominos
Hotel in Salinas in his youth, and next describes his entry into the Army and
OSS as the United States entered World War II. The explicit OSS bridge supports
a high-confidence identity. The sequence makes the hotel the best-supported
candidate for his last civilian employer before Army service, but it does not
explicitly exclude an intervening job or give a title. The employer claim is
therefore medium confidence and visibly qualified, not included as an
unqualified immediate affiliation.

The U.S. Army Special Operations Command History Office's documented history of
Team HERMIT identifies Raymond Compain of Coulanges, France, as Second
Lieutenant Herbert R. Brucker's bodyguard for the mission and describes Brucker
hiring him after the team landed in late May 1944. The exact uncommon name, the
index's `French` note and the direct OSS context support a high-confidence
identity and special-personnel classification. Bodyguard was his wartime OSS
role, not evidence of a pre-OSS employer, so it is used for identity only.

Every person received recorded NARA and CIA context checks, exact-name OSS and
meaningful variant searches, employment and occupation queries, and applicable
institutional, obituary, directory, newspaper, current Library of Congress and
archival discovery. A Library of Congress exact-name search for Lorna M Combs
returned a 20 January 1945 *Newport Navalog* OCR hit, but page-level review shows
the text names Lorna Slacomb. It was rejected. A modern Joseph F Comerford
obituary describes a man born in 1928 whose Army service began during the
Korean War and was rejected as a namesake. No authenticated NARA Catalog API
request was used.
