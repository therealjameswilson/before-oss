Culbertson Papers provides a direct identity bridge. It describes Culbertson
as chief of the Military Intelligence Service in the War Department and a

Army service from 1942 to 1945. This explicit institutional evidence, not the

The War Department Military Intelligence role is the best-supported immediate
pre-OSS affiliation, but the finding aid does not state a transfer date or
whether the assignments overlapped. It is therefore published as
`probable_immediate` and medium confidence rather than an unqualified fact.

The same finding aid separately documents:

- employment as professor and executive-faculty member at Georgetown
  University's School of Foreign Service, 1919-1956;
- private practice in international and administrative law in Washington in
  1934, without a named firm or end date;
- United States Tariff Commission service, 1917-1925; and
- diplomatic service as minister to Romania, 1925-1928, and ambassador to
  Chile, 1928-1933.

The Georgetown appointment is a named employer and produces this batch's one
new verified-employer profile. Its broad dates overlap the other roles, so it
is not silently labeled the last civilian employer. The law practice is
self-employment, while tariff and diplomatic work remain government
assignments. A reviewed 6 October 1933 *Evening Star* page independently

ambassadorship; it is supportive identity evidence, not the principal source
for the OSS link.
