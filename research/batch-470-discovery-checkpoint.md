## Sidney A Corderman pathway

McIntosh Laboratory's institutional memorial says Sidney Corderman joined OSS
in 1942 at age sixteen, remained in high school while continuing OSS work, was
drafted at eighteen, and continued in OSS as an Army communications-laboratory
technician. The exact-name and exact-private-identifier Army row independently
dates entry to 11 January 1944 as a private, records birth-year code 1925, and
carries student code 992. The sources therefore confirm the indexed Sidney A
Corderman and support high-school student status as his immediate pre-OSS
affiliation. The school is not named, and student status is not employment.

The adjacent no-middle-initial Sidney Corderman row remains a separate source
record and person entity. Its proximity, same Box 144, and exact base name make
it a plausible duplicate, but name similarity is not enough to merge it with
Sidney A Corderman. Both entities are assigned the duplicate-review group
`batch470-sidney-corderman-page93-rows10-11`; the unnumbered row requires
critical archival review.

transparent normalizer therefore classifies him as foreign or Allied military

`042f2062b0deec40860d9c183f10069dff27e5e01620e733814da4796adaebfe`.
Physical page 237 lists `Dossier individuel de Georges CORDEAU`, file
`GR 28 P 4 175 / 187`, dated 1943-1982, with related resistance file
`GR 16 P 142178`. The finding aid describes the series as individual files of
agents in intelligence networks and resistance movements.

Marcel Treville's explicitly tentative, source-referenced infiltration register
was checked only as corroboration. Physical page 171 identifies Georges Cordeau
as a wireless operator on the Proust/OSS/Midiron `Conge` mission and gives the
aliases George Courtois and Cottard, citing the same SHD file and NARA M1623.
Because the index has no date of birth or archival cross-reference, the combined
evidence supports a high-confidence rather than confirmed identity. It does not
establish a pre-OSS employer or immediate predecessor affiliation.
