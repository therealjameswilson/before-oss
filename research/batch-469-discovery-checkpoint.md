## Joseph D Coppock pathway

The Harry S. Truman Presidential Library's first-person Joseph D. Coppock oral
history provides a direct identity and chronology bridge. Coppock says he
became head of the Office of Price Administration's Chemicals and Drugs Branch
in early 1943, remained through the fall, and went to OSS in late 1943, first as
a civilian. He says OSS arranged an early-1944 naval commission as lieutenant
junior grade. This confirms the indexed identity and later commissioned naval
status and supports the OPA role as the explicit immediate pre-OSS government
assignment.

The linked Truman Library finding aid independently documents Coppock as a 1941
U.S. Department of Agriculture economist, a 1942 special assistant to the War
Production Board vice chairman, and a 1943 OPA price executive. These are
stored as earlier government assignments, not private-sector employers.

Coppock's signed 1940 NBER acknowledgment was downloaded from the stable NBER
chapter URL, checked at 216,745 bytes and SHA-256
`128052745df1d427fca184a94c676c6a2f378f8f9f7d52a5c96f7a437e76ea92`,
and visually inspected at physical page 15, printed page xii. He thanks
Swarthmore College for granting a leave of absence and signs as Financial
Research Staff at the National Bureau of Economic Research and with the
University of California. Swarthmore and NBER are published as documented
earlier employment; the University of California is only a professional
affiliation because no role is stated. The evidence shows concurrent roles, so
none is marked as the single last civilian employer before government service.

The index row has no printed rank or identifier. The commissioned-naval
classification comes from Coppock's explicit later OSS chronology, not from a
silent interpretation of the blank index rank.
