## Nick R Cooky / Nick R. Kukich

Two official institutional histories establish that the unusual printed form
belongs to Marine Sergeant Nick R. Kukich. The National Park Service identifies
Marine Sergeant (later lieutenant) Nick R. `Cooky` Kukich, age 27, a coal miner
from Ohio who received OSS paramilitary and radio training. Its note explicitly
states that the Marine Corps history mistook his nickname for his surname.

The official Marine Corps monograph uses the index form `Nick R. Cooky`. It
identifies him as the TANK mission's Gunnery Sergeant and USMC radio operator
from 17 November 1943, records his later commission as a second lieutenant,
and describes him as a former miner and truck driver from Dilles Bottom, Ohio.
Its roster again lists Second Lieutenant Nick R. Cooky for Italy and Albania.

The rare exact index form, printed Sergeant rank, explicit NPS correction and
official Marine history support a confirmed identity while preserving both
name forms. Because the index captures him as a sergeant, the profile classifies
the indexed service status as enlisted Marine Corps and does not retroactively
label that row commissioned. The Marine Corps is the best-supported immediate
pre-OSS military pathway. Miner and truck driver are earlier occupations only;
no mine, hauling company, employer or exact chronology is inferred.
