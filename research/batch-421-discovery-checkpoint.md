## Review method

The complete source page was inspected from a 150 dpi render before research.
The ten rows were then checked against the complete 9,200,232-row Army Serial
Number Merged File, its fixed-width technical documentation and rendered NARA
occupational-code pages. Exact names, meaningful spelling and punctuation
variants, the two private identifiers supplied by the index, OSS and CIA
context, employment and occupation terms, obituaries, institutional sources,
current Library of Congress discovery and broader archival discovery were
reviewed in stages. Search snippets, genealogy-only pages, modern people
finders and name-only biographies were not accepted as final evidence.

No raw Army row, full private identifier, authenticated API payload, home
address, phone number, relative detail or family-sensitive data is retained
here. The user-supplied NARA API key was not used.

## Source-specific cautions

- The Army merged file is incomplete, especially for officers. A missing row
  is not negative proof.
- Army occupation code 001 identifies a broad category, not an employer,
  workplace, exact accounting role or employment dates.
- Calvin L Clark's different-identifier row remains a separate person unless
  the personnel file directly reconciles the conflict.
- The sole exact-name Army rows for Andrew, Clayton, Dorothy and Douglas do not
  satisfy the enhanced common-name rule. Their occupations, locations and
  assignments remain private.
- Six Army records and several further biographies for Clyde Clark remain
  separate. No convenient candidate was selected.
- The Vassar biography and a contemporary 1943 report strongly identify writer
  Eleanor Clark as an OSS employee. They do not establish that the unqualified
  Box 126 name belongs to that writer, so none of her publishing, translation,
  political or educational history is transferred.
- An OSS personnel-file index row establishes that a file exists, not by itself
  the person's exact role or the chronology of every outside record.
