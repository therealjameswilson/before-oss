## Review method

The complete source page was inspected from a 150 dpi render before research.
The ten rows were then checked against the complete 9,200,232-row Army Serial
Number Merged File, its fixed-width technical documentation and rendered NARA
occupational-code pages. The seven private identifiers supplied by the index
were compared without retaining them in this checkpoint. Exact names,
meaningful punctuation and suffix variants, OSS and CIA context, employment
and occupation terms, obituaries, institutional sources, current Library of
Congress discovery and broader archival discovery were reviewed in stages.
Search snippets, genealogy-only pages, modern people finders and name-only
biographies were not accepted as final evidence.

No raw Army row, full private identifier, authenticated API payload, home
address, phone number, relative detail or family-sensitive data is retained
here. The user-supplied NARA API key was not used.

## Source-specific cautions

- The Army merged file is incomplete, especially for officers. A missing row
  is not negative proof and does not identify a service branch.
- The occupation codes are broad historical categories, not employers,
  workplaces, exact specialties or employment dates.
- The selected first Ernest D Clark record has a malformed grade field. The
  value in the documented occupation columns remains publishable only with a
  record-quality warning; the malformed grade is not corrected.
- Frederick L Clark's selected record contains blank grade and branch fields
  plus internally anomalous date-of-birth data. The exact name and private
  identifier support identity, but the occupation is qualified and no
  personnel category is inferred.
- The second Ernest D Clark index row remains separate from the first. Its
  private identifier selects a different-middle-initial Army record, whose
  occupation, location and assignment fields are not transferred.
- One Francis V Clark, seventeen Frank J Clark, forty-two George H Clark,
  fifty George E Clark and two George O Clark Jr. exact-name Army rows were
  reviewed as separate people. Only the supplied exact private identifiers
  select George H and one Ernest D row.
- The Ellery Harding Clark Jr. naval biography documents a real World War II
  officer, but its middle initial conflicts with the index and no source links
  its service number or career to OSS. It remains a private rejected lead.
- An OSS personnel-file index row establishes that a file exists, not by
  itself the person's exact role or the chronology of every outside record.
