## Francis L Coolidge

The distinctive index entry resolves at high confidence to Francis Lowell
Coolidge. National Park Service histories identify Captain Francis L.
“Frank” Coolidge as an OSS instructor in 1943 and as Peter Ortiz's
second-in-command on UNION II, with a supporting archival citation to RG 226,
Entry 161, Box 2, Folder 31. Smithsonian independently identifies Francis
Lowell Coolidge as a United States Army Air Forces captain who requested
transfer to OSS in 1943.

The Army Air Forces is therefore the best-supported immediate pre-OSS
military affiliation, with explicit-immediate temporal evidence and high claim
confidence. This is not a civilian employer.

A Town of Mattapoisett historical inventory identifies a brief stockbroking
career after Harvard, then French Foreign Legion service from 1931 through
1936. Smithsonian independently corroborates his Foreign Legion combat
experience. The Legion service is a high-confidence earlier military
affiliation. Stockbroker is a medium-confidence earlier occupation only: no
brokerage, client, self-employment status, exact date, or last-civilian-employer
sequence is identified.

## Harold J Coolidge

The exact index form resolves at high confidence to Harold Jefferson Coolidge
Jr. A Washington Post obituary states that he was on the staff of Harvard's
Museum of Comparative Zoology from 1929 through 1946 and moved to Washington
in 1940 to join OSS. It separately calls him a former curator of mammals.
Harvard MCZbase corroborates the full name, variants, life dates, expedition
relationships, and specimen-collection activity through 1940.

The museum is published as both the explicit immediate pre-OSS affiliation and
the last civilian employer at high confidence. The active pre-OSS work is
bounded to his 1940 move; the profile also preserves that the formal museum
appointment continued through 1946. No military rank or commissioned status is
inferred.

## Philip H Coombs

The distinctive index entry resolves at high confidence to economist and
educator Philip Hall Coombs. The Washington Post places labor-economics
teaching at Williams College before Army Air Forces service with OSS in Italy.
Contemporary Authors independently sequences Williams teaching, later work as
an economist with the U.S. Office of Price Administration just before World
War II, and wartime OSS service. The State Department Historian corroborates
the full name and life dates but contributes no prewar employment claim.

OPA is published as a medium-confidence, probable-immediate government
assignment because it is the latest named prewar role; the source does not say
that Coombs transferred directly from OPA to OSS. Williams College is
published, also at medium confidence, as the best-documented last civilian
employer before the intervening federal assignment and wartime service. The
profile does not conflate either role with his Army Air Forces pathway and does
not infer a commissioned or enlisted grade.
