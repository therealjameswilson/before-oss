## Review method

PDF page 86 was rendered at 150 dpi and visually inspected before research.
Every printed name, initial, blank rank, private identifier or blank, box, note
and location in the ten-row cohort was compared with the immutable database.
Nine rows print Box 132 and Leonard Cohen prints Box 133; all ten print location
`230/86/28/07` and no rank. Seven private identifiers remain research-only and
are masked in every public output. Repeated Cohen surnames are not treated as
evidence of kinship, duplication or a shared biography.

The complete official Army merged file was scanned across 9,200,232
fixed-width records. Literal comparisons tested all seven printed identifiers
without silently padding, truncating or rewriting them. Thomas B Cogley, Colby
A Cogswell, Albert Cohen, Arthur A Cohen and Harry Cohen each have one exact
identifier-and-name match. Harold Cohen and Leonard Cohen have no literal
identifier match. Exact-name candidate counts were also reviewed for all ten
people; common-name candidates were not selected without the required identity
bridge.

Official NARA record-layout and code-list documents were reviewed before any
Army field was interpreted. All five identifier-confirmed men entered the Army
as privates. Their categories were recorded as follows:

- Thomas B Cogley, 13 August 1943: `Electrical machinery and accessories
  manufacturing occupations, n.e.c.`
- Colby A Cogswell, 7 June 1943: `Salesmen, stock and bond`
- Albert Cohen, 9 February 1942: `Warehousing, storekeeping, handling, loading,
  unloading, and related occupations, n.e.c.`
- Arthur A Cohen, 3 November 1942: `Foremen, n.e.c.`
- Harry Cohen, 30 April 1941: `Managers and officials, n.e.c.`

These are historical civilian-occupation categories, not named employers.
They do not identify a company, client, workplace, ownership status or direct
transition to OSS.

Colby A Cogswell produced the only documented employer pathway in the cohort.
A Dartmouth Alumni Magazine obituary supplies his full name, class year and
wartime OSS service in Paris. A contemporary March 1944 Dartmouth war directory
lists Colby A. Cogswell in the Army of the United States. A 1954 class notice,
based on information from Northern Trust, says that he joined Northern Trust in
1949 after ten years with the First Boston Corporation. Read with the official
Army-entry date, the source supports First Boston as the best-supported last
civilian employer before Army service. It does not state the exact leave or
separation date, and its literal ten-year summary spans wartime service.
Accordingly, First Boston is published at medium confidence with
`probable_immediate` temporal basis, not as an explicit immediate pre-OSS
affiliation. No role title is borrowed from the separate Army occupation code.

Dartmouth College is published for Cogswell only as a student relationship.
The United States Army is published separately as a military assignment; the
official entry record documents private grade, and the contemporary directory
documents Army status. The obituary's generic word `officer` is not treated as
proof of a military commission. His exact later Army rank, unit and Army-to-OSS
transition remain open archival questions.

Mary M Coghlan, David C Cohen, Harold Cohen, Jean Cohen and Leonard Cohen remain
unresolved after staged official, institutional, newspaper, directory,
obituary and archival discovery. The famous writer Arthur Allen Cohen, the
Canadian singer-songwriter Leonard Cohen, a British military-intelligence
Leonard Cohen candidate, and a Harold Cohen associated with the 4th Armored
Division were rejected because none supplied the required index-person bridge.
The Dartmouth registrar page presented an access challenge; it was not used as
evidence, and no access restriction was bypassed.

Research for every person also covered official NARA and CIA context,
exact-name OSS searches and meaningful variants, employment and occupation
queries, institutional archives, obituaries, directories, newspapers, current
Library of Congress discovery and other archival searches. Search snippets,
unsourced genealogy, modern people finders, relative-centered material and
name-only biographies were rejected as final evidence. Sensitive modern
details were not retained.

The user-supplied NARA Catalog API credential was not used. No raw Army row,
full private identifier, live API response, home address, phone number,
living-relative detail or unrelated sensitive data is retained.

## Source-specific cautions

- The Army merged file is incomplete and is neither an officer, Navy, Marine
  Corps nor comprehensive OSS register. Non-hits are not negative proof.
- Historical Army-entry occupation categories identify no employer and do not
  establish whether the recorded status immediately preceded OSS service.
- The First Boston chronology is reconstructed from a retrospective ten-year
  summary and a separate official Army-entry date. It is visibly qualified and
  excluded from default high-confidence employer analytics.
- Dartmouth attendance is a student relationship, not employment.
- Army service is a military assignment, not a civilian employer.
- Private grade at Army entry does not establish Cogswell's later rank; the
  obituary's generic use of `officer` is not a commission record.
- A printed identifier is never silently altered to select a namesake.
- Shared surnames do not establish family relationships or duplicate people.
