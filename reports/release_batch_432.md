## Research

rank, private identifier or blank, box, note and location in the cohort was

prints `Lt` for Charles K Cloninger and Evelyne Clopet and no rank for the

A complete transient scan of 9,200,232 fixed-width Army merged-file records
used literal private-identifier comparison only for two eligible eight-digit
values. Joseph F Clinton's identifier selects the sole exact-name row and
supports a confirmed enlisted Army identity. Official Army code documentation
labels his entry occupation `Embalmers and undertakers`; that observation is
published at medium confidence with uncertain timing. It names no employer,
funeral home, location or exact role and is not marked as an immediate
affiliation.

John L Clive has no exact-name Army row, but that non-hit does not overturn his
more specific Harvard institutional chronology. The existing evidence
continues to support a high-confidence identity as John Leonard Clive, an Army
affiliation immediately before OSS and earlier University of North Carolina
student status. The Army is a military assignment, the university is an
educational relationship, and neither is presented as a civilian employer.

Edward J Clinton's seven-digit index value and Charles K Cloninger's six-digit
value were preserved exactly; neither was padded, truncated or otherwise
forced into an Army identifier. Four exact-name Edward rows remain rejected.
The sole exact-name Charles row was an enlisted private entering in 1942 and
conflicts with the indexed Lieutenant rank without an identifier bridge. A
privately compiled Artillery Officer Candidate School roster lists Charles K
Cloninger in class 93 of 1943. The distinctive name and officer training are
consistent with the index, but the roster provides no OSS, Box 130 or private-
identifier bridge. It therefore supports only a visibly qualified probable
identity at medium confidence, not an employer or pre-OSS affiliation.

Official French sources provide the cohort's strongest new pathway. A French
Ministry of the Armed Forces profile was downloaded, hashed and visually
inspected. It gives the full name Evelyne Claire Valentine Clopet, the rank
`sous-lieutenant`, volunteer service in the Corps feminin des transmissions,
entry into the BCRA in London in February 1944, integration into Plan Sussex
and radio work under OSS orders. A Gendarmerie nationale article independently
identifies her as a BCRA radio operator in Sussex, and the Service historique
de la Defense catalogs four related file references. The rare exact name,
matching officer rank and explicit OSS context support confirmed identity.

The evidence supports the BCRA as her strongly date-bounded immediate military
affiliation and the CFT as an earlier Allied military affiliation. Neither is
a civilian employer. The sources do not establish a distinct OSS recruitment
date or whether BCRA membership continued concurrently, so the BCRA
relationship is not labeled explicitly immediate. The four SHD files remain
archival follow-up rather than reviewed evidence.

The Army scan produced no exact-name row for Margery T Clinton, Frank T Cloak,
Jan Cloesmeyer, Ida M Clohan, Elton Clohecy or Evelyne Clopet. Those non-hits
are not negative proof because the file is incomplete and is neither an
officer, Navy, Allied nor comprehensive OSS register. Jan Cloesmeyer and Elton
Clohecy retain multiple unbridged candidates and remain ambiguous. Edward J
Clinton, Margery T Clinton, Frank T Cloak and Ida M Clohan remain unresolved.
Candidate nationality, education, occupation and later biography were not
transferred from namesakes.

Exact-name OSS and CIA searches, meaningful variants, employment and
